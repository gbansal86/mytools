## Role 1 — Creator review
As an educational YouTube producer, check the 3-second hook, pacing, visual continuity, one clear objective, accurate examples, and whether every animation earns its screen time. Learn from successful short-form teaching principles; do not imitate or impersonate a specific creator.

## Role 2 — First-time viewer review
Test whether the lesson can be restated after one viewing with sound off and then with sound on. Check text at actual phone size; trace every arrow from an explicit source to a meaningful destination. Remove distractions, too many examples and unexplained metaphors.

## Locked production style for the pilot
- Combine high-quality **infographic sequences AND a moving 3D/3D-style mind map** in one video.
- Use approved visual references as targets for geometry, icon language, material quality, lighting, depth, colours and typography; recreate their objects as editable layers/3D scenes. Never silently replace them with primitive rectangles, dots and straight arrows.
- Give visual evidence of flowing data, animated model pathways, changing examples, expanding spatial branches and word-synced callouts. A simple pan/zoom of a static image alone is insufficient.
- Render readable annotation text independently of generative imagery. Use simple relevant callouts, not tiny text baked into an image.
- A neural-network/brain symbol is an illustrative metaphor, not a literal biological brain.
- Warm conversational narration must be obtained, listened to, timed and checked before calling the video final. Never claim a silent preview has voice or voice-sync.
- Preserve scientific accuracy and uncertainty: trained models ordinarily use learned parameters at inference; outputs can be wrong.

## Ordered workflow and approval gates
1. Review learning objective and factual claims. Show the proposed educational script.
2. Lock a natural voice script and measure an actual narrated recording. If voice generation/recording is unavailable, STOP at the clearly named silent-prototype gate and say what is missing.
3. Storyboard every scene and match approved visual references with close-up comparisons. Get approval of **one polished representative scene** before mass-rendering.
4. Build editable assets and preserve approved reference artwork. Create actual motion: paths, particles, icon travel, state transitions, spatial camera/branch movement.
5. Sync annotations and captions to measured narration timings; mix authorized music/SFX.
6. Render a phone-sized review and audit screenshots across scenes; check motion and exact voice timing.
7. Export the final 1080x1920 9:16 video and run technical, factual, rights and readability QA. A draft animatic cannot pass this gate.
8. Publish script, editable source, prompts, assets with verified rights, QA, final video (if size/rights permit) and resume log under `Digital Videos/AI Video Creation` in GitHub on a feature branch/PR. Verify all uploaded files by readback; never claim main is updated until merged.

## Hard STOP conditions
- An approved infographic or 3D mind map is missing from the actual finished animation.
- The visual looks like a basic PowerPoint slide, with primitive boxes replacing approved artwork.
- A final MP4 lacks narration, readable captions or genuine animated flows.
- A model/tool cannot produce requested voice or 3D movement and the limitation has not been disclosed.
- A file was only created locally but is described as already pushed to GitHub.
- A sandbox download link or GitHub link has not been verified.
